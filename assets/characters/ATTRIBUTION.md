# Character concepts

AI-generated, unofficial, branding-inspired character concepts supplied by the project owner (2026-10-09/11). These concepts are not affiliated with or endorsed by Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Alibaba, Meta or Mistral AI. Names and colors are used only as costume themes; trademarks belong to their owners. No model/provider affiliation is inferred from a session's harness.

Original files, copied without modification into `source/`: `claude.png`, `codex.png`, `gemini.png`, `qwen.png`, `llama.png`, `mistral.png`, `manifest.json` and `README.md`, from the owner's `asenq-character-movements` concept pack. `operator.png` is the original `exec-9b80f5c9-ab98-49d0-9544-fbbb9bca6c5f.png`; it depicts the project owner and is used with permission.

Provider sheets have six variant rows and eight pose columns, ordered: `idle_a`, `idle_b`, `walk_a`, `walk_b`, `work_a`, `work_b`, `send_a`, `send_b`. The operator sheet has one large reference plus eight poses in two rows of four in that same order.

Run `node scripts/derive-sprites.mjs` to derive both detailed terminal and browser character data, `--check` to compare both deterministic outputs, `--preview <outfit|operator|neutral>` to inspect terminal half-block frames, or `--png <outfit|operator|neutral> <path>` to render the browser grid at 4× nearest-neighbour scale. The generator requires ffmpeg and ffprobe on PATH. Parameters and sanctioned head/neutral corrections live in `derive.json`; generated `src/viz/cast-detailed.ts` and `web/city/assets/characters.json` must not be hand-edited. Raw raster references are excluded from the npm package.

Detailed frames use a 20×24 pixel canvas, a shared scale per variant and a strict 2% opaque-area clipping limit. Per the owner's “Fit affected variants” choice, only variants exceeding that limit at `23 / idle_a bbox height` use a smaller shared scale: a deterministic 40-iteration binary search finds the maximal scale satisfying every pose. The generator reports fitted scales to stderr; preview artwork alone goes to stdout. A full six-variant preview occupies 168 columns and 85 output rows (operator: 168 columns and 15 rows); allow one extra terminal row for the final newline.

Near-white source pixels (`value >= 0.85`, `saturation <= 0.25`) start in a separate median-cut box within the same 12-color palette, preserving white clothing instead of merging it into skin. Downsampling prioritizes the existing saturated feature rule, then near-white coverage of at least 20%, then the most frequent color. Neutral garments use hue 8°–48°, saturation at least 0.6 and value at least 0.35; `neutralKeep` explicitly protects source brown face/shadow palette entries that overlap this garment band. Compact neutral scarves/collars use graphite `#6a6e77`; hair and skin traits remain the original Mistral colors.

Browser frames use the `web` profile: 32×48 pixels, baseline 47, horizontal anchor 15.5 and an 18-row head-region cap. Both profiles derive from the same decoded sheets and frame segmentation; each keeps its own head corrections and shared per-variant scale fit. Source palette indices and the shared neutral exclusions are applied before the browser-only spatial preservation described below. The browser JSON contains `width`, `height`, `poses` and `outfits`, with 43 variants and 344 frames including neutral.

Web-only corrections use `followHead`: the head region follows the top opaque row of the central silhouette, excluding raised hands at the edges, and spans one quarter of the fitted figure height, capped at 18 rows. A fixed absolute top-18-row region misses the heads of small fitted figures, including the capped Codex variant. Visual inspection of all eight outfit grids added browser corrections for Claude's third idle pose, Llama's first idle pose, Mistral's sixth work pose, and the operator's work pose, in addition to the existing sanctioned corrections.

Browser neutral frames preserve hair and skin spatially in this head region, rather than recoloring the whole palette: the sixth Mistral variant uses the same orange entries for its hair and jacket. Original head colors and unaffected source colors take priority in the browser's 12-color palette, with at least one slot reserved for neutral garment shades; rare overflow colors are mapped to the nearest retained color. Thus the orange hair remains orange while the jacket becomes neutral gray. Terminal derivation, palette indices, head corrections and neutral exclusions remain unchanged.
